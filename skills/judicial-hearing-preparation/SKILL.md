# Judicial and hearing preparation

Keep sourced court and judge information separate from simulation behavior.

## Court profile

Collect jurisdiction, subject-matter limits, local rules, standing procedures, filing requirements, scheduling, service, hearing practices, remote appearance rules, forms, and controlling appellate hierarchy. Prefer official sources and state the current-through date.

## Judge profile

Collect official biography, standing orders, courtroom procedures, relevant written decisions grouped by issue and posture, appellate treatment, and lawfully available argument materials. State the sample size and source coverage.

Do not infer protected traits, motives, or temperament. Do not predict a future ruling. Any outcome counts must be contextualized by case type and procedural posture.

## Hearing simulation

Require the hearing type, user's side, forum, requested relief, time limit, desired exercise, filing or outline, verified authority set, and important record citations. Identify missing inputs before beginning.

Supported exercises include rapid-fire questions, full timed argument, interruption mode, weakest-issue drill, record-citation drill, remedy or jurisdiction drill, and appellate-panel mode.

Ask questions grounded in the supplied record, authorities, procedure, profile, and adversarial risk analysis. Do not give the ideal answer until the user answers or asks to skip.

## Debrief

Save or return the transcript, unanswered questions, unsupported answers, missed authorities or record cites, concessions, framing risks, speaking feedback, substantive research gaps, and follow-up tasks. Do not impersonate a real judge or present speculative behavior as fact.
